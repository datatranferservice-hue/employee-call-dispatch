04 — EXACT CALL FLOW

Use this seven-step conversation structure.

1. Permission:

SAY:
“Hi, this is [YOUR NAME] with Sentinel Zero. I know this is a cold call—can I take 20 seconds and tell you exactly why I called?”

2. Reason:

SAY:
“We help small and midsize businesses find security gaps that usually get missed until an insurance renewal, customer requirement, or incident forces the issue. I’m not calling to sell you software.”

3. Find ownership:

ASK:
“Who handles cybersecurity or cyber-insurance decisions there?”

4. Qualify:

ASK 2–3:
“Do you have anyone formally responsible for cybersecurity today?”
“Has an insurer, customer, regulator or partner asked for security documentation?”
“If you found a serious gap, who would decide what gets fixed?”

5. Connect the problem: Repeat back the specific issue they mentioned in plain language.

6. Close for the next conversation:

SAY:
“Based on what you told me, the next step is a short fit call—not a contract. We’ll identify the biggest concerns and whether a deeper review makes sense. What works better, earlier in the day or later?”

7. Log it: Save contact details, the pain point, decision maker, timing and promised next action in the Sales Desk before calling the next company.

For Mobile Device Security, Ongoing Advisory, Broker Partner scripts and objection buttons, use the Caller Sales Desk. The script changes automatically when you choose the offer.

05 — LOG THE OUTCOME

Every meaningful call must end with a defined next status.

Booked

Decision maker agreed to a fit call/security review. Record date/time and what matters to them.

Follow-up

They are not ready today but gave a real reason or timing. Record the exact callback date and reason.

Send information

Do not just email a generic brochure. Ask what matters most, send the matching Business or Mobile page, then set a follow-up.

Wrong person

Get the correct decision maker’s name/title whenever possible. Do not mark it dead just because the receptionist answered.

Not interested

Thank them and move on. Do not argue. If they request no more calls, honor the opt-out.

Qualified opportunity

There is a clear business problem, authority, urgency or requirement. Escalate to the owner/closer with clean notes.

07 — WHEN TO ESCALATE

Bring the owner/closer in when the conversation becomes commercial or technical.

Escalate immediately when:

• They ask for a proposal or contract
• They want pricing for a defined scope
• There is a breach/active incident
• Insurance renewal or customer deadline is close
• They have a compliance/security questionnaire problem
• A multi-location or larger organization is interested
• They want technical proof, architecture or detailed remediation

Caller keeps ownership when:

• Finding the right decision maker
• Getting permission for the conversation
• Qualifying the need
• Sending the correct landing page
• Booking the fit call
• Recording notes and next action
• Following up at the agreed time

Management rule: If attempts are high but decision-maker conversations are low, fix targeting/gatekeeper technique. If conversations are high but bookings are low, fix offer/script. If bookings are high but wins are low, fix qualification, sales call, pricing or offer—not dial volume.
09 — CALLER RULES

Protect the company while selling.

DO:
Use plain language. Be accurate. Ask permission. Ask questions. Keep notes. Respect opt-outs. Say “I don’t want to guess; I’ll get the right answer” when you do not know.
DO NOT:
Claim a prospect was hacked when you do not know. Promise guaranteed protection. Invent compliance results. Pretend to be their insurer, IT company or government agency. Pressure someone after an explicit no. Make up pricing or technical answers.

AI-generated/prerecorded outbound calling can trigger additional consent and telemarketing requirements. Until the automated telephone lane has been deliberately cleared for a campaign, employees should use the approved human-calling workflow and company compliance rules.
