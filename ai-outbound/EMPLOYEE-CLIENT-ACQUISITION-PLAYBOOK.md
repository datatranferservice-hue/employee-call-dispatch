# Sentinel Zero Employee Client Acquisition Playbook

## Purpose
Your job is not to explain cybersecurity for five minutes. Your job is to create the next qualified conversation.

**Primary cold-call objective:** permission for 20 seconds -> identify a relevant problem -> reach the decision maker -> schedule a fit call/security review -> log the next action.

## Start here
1. Open the protected Caller Sales Desk at `/sales`.
2. Choose one offer before dialing.
3. Call a real approved target.
4. Ask for the person who handles cybersecurity or cyber-insurance decisions.
5. Use the script and ask 2-3 qualification questions.
6. Close for a short fit call, not a full contract.
7. Save every meaningful prospect immediately.
8. Follow up exactly when promised.

## Offers
### Business Security Review
Default offer for most business cold calls. Best for medical practices, construction companies, law firms and professional services.

### Ongoing Cyber Advisory
For businesses needing security leadership, risk prioritization, documentation, accountability, insurance preparation or cybersecurity oversight without a full-time security executive.

### Mobile Device Security
For individuals or workforces using phones for email, banking, passwords, authentication, work accounts, backups and sensitive communications.

### Insurance Broker Partnership
For brokers whose insured clients are delayed by cybersecurity controls during underwriting or renewal.

## Who to call
- Healthcare: owner, practice manager, administrator, IT/security lead.
- Construction: owner, operations, finance, IT.
- Law firms: managing partner, administrator, IT.
- Professional services: owner, COO, IT, compliance.
- Insurance brokers: principal, producer, cyber-practice lead.

## Core business script
**Permission**
> Hi, this is [YOUR NAME] with Sentinel Zero. I know this is a cold call—can I take 20 seconds and tell you exactly why I called?

**Reason**
> We help small and midsize businesses find security gaps that usually get missed until an insurance renewal, customer requirement, or incident forces the issue. I’m not calling to sell you software.

**Find ownership**
> Who handles cybersecurity or cyber-insurance decisions there?

**Qualification questions**
- Do you have anyone formally responsible for cybersecurity today?
- Has an insurer, customer, regulator or partner asked for security documentation?
- If you found a serious gap, who would decide what gets fixed?

**Close**
> Based on what you told me, the next step is a short fit call—not a contract. We’ll identify the biggest concerns and whether a deeper review makes sense. What works better, earlier in the day or later?

## Call outcomes
Every meaningful call must end in a defined status:
- Booked
- Follow-up
- Send information + scheduled follow-up
- Wrong person / find decision maker
- Not interested
- Do-not-call / opt-out
- Qualified opportunity / escalate

## Follow-up rules
- Never “send and hope.” If you send information, schedule a next touch.
- If the decision maker is unavailable, get a name and callback window.
- Tie future follow-up to a real event when possible: renewal, audit, customer request, contract, budget cycle or planning date.
- For booked meetings, capture the pain point so the closer does not have to rediscover it.
- Honor explicit opt-outs.

## Escalate to the owner/closer when
- They ask for a proposal or contract.
- They ask for pricing on a defined scope.
- There is an active breach or incident.
- A renewal, customer or compliance deadline is close.
- They have a security questionnaire/compliance problem.
- A multi-location or larger organization is interested.
- They ask for detailed technical proof or remediation design.

## Caller owns
- Finding the decision maker.
- Getting permission for the conversation.
- Qualifying the need.
- Sending the correct landing page.
- Booking the fit call.
- Recording clean notes.
- Following up on time.

## Management scoreboard
Track the funnel, not just call count:
1. Attempts
2. Human connects
3. Decision-maker conversations
4. Booked fit calls
5. Qualified opportunities
6. Proposals
7. Paid wins
8. Source and caller for every win

**Diagnosis rule:** high attempts + low decision-maker conversations = targeting/gatekeeper problem. High conversations + low bookings = script/offer problem. High bookings + low wins = qualification/sales/pricing/offer problem.

## Caller rules
### Do
- Use plain language.
- Ask permission.
- Ask questions before explaining.
- Keep accurate notes.
- Respect opt-outs.
- Say you will get the correct answer instead of guessing.

### Do not
- Claim a prospect was hacked without evidence.
- Promise guaranteed protection.
- Invent compliance results.
- Pretend to be an insurer, government agency or the prospect’s IT company.
- Pressure someone after an explicit no.
- Invent pricing or technical answers.

## Daily checklist
- Open `/playbook` and `/sales`.
- Choose one offer and one target segment.
- Start real calls before reorganizing lists.
- Log every meaningful prospect.
- Complete prior promised follow-ups.
- Escalate qualified opportunities quickly.
- End the session with attempts -> connects -> decision makers -> bookings -> qualified opportunities -> wins.
